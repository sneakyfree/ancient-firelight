# Ancient Firelight — Style Bible v1

## The formal joke (say it once, in episode one, then never again)
A stick-figure channel about the origin of art is not a budget compromise. **The first art was crude.** The style *is* the subject. We are not doing cheap animation; we are doing archaeologically faithful animation.

This buys us: near-zero production cost, exemption from YouTube's AI-disclosure regime (animation is exempt), and a look nobody can accuse of being slop — because it is deliberately, visibly hand-made.

## The rule that protects the whole pipeline
**The cast is hand-drawn. Always. Never generated.**
A stick figure drawn once is perfectly consistent forever, by construction. Character LoRAs drift; hands drift; faces drift. Every AI-faceless channel dies of visual drift around episode 20. We sidestep it by not playing.

The 5090 does **world**, not **cast**: backgrounds, megafauna, tools, maps, cave walls, thumbnails — via one style LoRA, in a locked house style.

## Palette (from the artifact; these are the channel's colours)
| Role | Hex | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Soot (ground) | `#14100E` | backgrounds, night |
| Ember (accent, pigment) | `#C4451F` | fire, ochre, the one bold colour |
| Firelight (ink) | `#EDE4D6` | figures, text |
| Ash (muted) | `#9C8F80` | secondary figures, far background |
| Limestone | `#DDD5C7` | cave wall, day scenes |
| Verdigris | `#84A98C` | rare — used only for "what we actually know" beats |

**Ember is the only saturated colour on the channel.** Fire, ochre, blood, and nothing else. When ember appears, it means something.

## The cast (draw these once, in Krita or Inkscape, as layered puppets)
Each character: **5–8 body poses + 6 head/expression variants**, exported as layered SVG/PNG with separable limbs for cutout rigging in DaVinci Resolve.

| Character | Who | Read |
|---|---|---|
| **The Drummer** | tapped her foot for no reason evolution would approve of | restless; always slightly apart from the group |
| **The Singer** | held a note and bent it | head tilted back; the only one looking up |
| **The Painter** | the hand on the wall at Muna | one hand always ochre-red to the wrist |
| **The Group** | the twenty around the fire | drawn as a silhouette *mass*, not individuals — cheap, and it makes the one who stands up read instantly |
| **The Narrator's hand** | pointer/annotator | appears to underline, circle, cross out — our version of History Matters' placards |

Design constraints: no facial features beyond a suggestion (avoids the uncanny-valley problem History Matters solved by giving characters placards instead of mouths). Bodies are 5 strokes max. Silhouettes must read at thumbnail size.

## The one signature move
**The Painter's hand.** Every episode, somewhere, a hand presses to a wall and pigment sprays around it. It is the channel's logo, its transition, and its sign-off. It came from the real 67,800-year-old artifact — fingertips tapered to points, like claws.

## Motion
Cutout puppets + parallax + Ken Burns. **No per-shot AI video** — 120 shots of drifting style is exactly how the slop look happens. One optional "hero shot" per episode via local Wan 2.2 or LTX if it genuinely earns it.

Firelight flicker is a global warm-light overlay with low-frequency noise — cheap, and it ties every night scene together.

## Type
EB Garamond (the same face as the book — brand continuity across Grant's whole body of work). Mono for on-screen data/dates/citations only.

## Sources on screen
Every hard claim gets a small citation in the corner, mono, ash-coloured. This is not decoration — it is the moat. The channel's whole strategic position is *"the history of how we got the story wrong,"* and you cannot hold that position without showing your work.
