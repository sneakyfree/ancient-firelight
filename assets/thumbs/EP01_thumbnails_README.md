# EP01 thumbnails — three concepts

Title they pair with: **What Did Ancient Humans Do at Night?**
Rule followed: the thumbnail *adds* a hook the title doesn't state — it never repeats the title.

| File | Text | Angle | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `EP01_B_it_wasnt_the_lions.png` | "It wasn't the *lions.*" | Contrarian — eyes in the dark, but the twist is the danger wasn't what you think | **★ Launch with this.** Highest curiosity gap; pairs with the title's question and the episode genuinely pays it off |
| `EP01_A_after_dark.png` | "AFTER *DARK*" | Atmospheric / premium | Safe alternate. Cleanest, most brandable; swap to this if B feels too oblique |
| `EP01_C_someone_always_awake.png` | "Someone was *always awake.*" | The payload, stated | Cerebral. Save as a 48-hour swap-test if B underperforms |

## Recommendation
**Launch with B.** It creates the strongest gap: the title asks "what did they do at night," the thumbnail says "it wasn't the lions" — and the viewer has to click to find out what the danger (and the answer) really was. The episode delivers it honestly: the fire-vs-predators story is unproven, and the real night defense was that someone was always awake. B is the hook; C's line is literally the answer — which is why C works as the follow-up test, not the opener.

## How to A/B test (built into YouTube now)
Studio → Content → click the video → **Test & compare** (thumbnail test): upload B, A, and C; YouTube rotates them and keeps the winner automatically. Use it — packaging is where the views are won.
