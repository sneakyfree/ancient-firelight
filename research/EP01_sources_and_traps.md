# EP01 — verified sources + the trap list

Every number in the script traces here. Fact-checked 2026-07-15/16 against primary sources.

## The verdict on the standard story
| Claim everyone repeats | Status |
|---|---|
| Fire held back the dark | **PARTLY** — habitual fire only ~400 ka; the predator half is unevidenced |
| Night became a space not about survival | **THEORETICAL** — Dunbar's time-budget model, not a measurement |
| That's where storytelling was born | **NOT SUPPORTED** — Wiessner explicitly forbids the projection |
| That's where the modern mind was made | **UNSUPPORTED** — nearest study (Smith 2017) calls itself "largely correlational" |

**We used the honest version. The collapse is the content.**

## Load-bearing quotes (verbatim — do not paraphrase on screen)
- **Packer et al. 2011**, *PLoS ONE* 6(7):e22285 — abstract, sentence 2: *"Nocturnal carnivores are widely believed to have played an important role in human evolution, driving the need for night-time shelter, the control of fire and our innate fear of darkness. However, no empirical data are available on the effects of darkness on the risks of predation in humans."* → **It's a MOONLIGHT study. Zero fire data.** 1,000+ victims 1988–2009 Tanzania; attacks 2–4× higher in the 10 days after full moon.
- **Wrangham 2017**, *Current Anthropology* 58(S16):S303 — the bridge: *"Control of fire is the obvious possibility."* Also, on his own cooking hypothesis: *"At the present time no solution is satisfactory."*
- **Yetish et al. 2015**, *Curr Biol* 25(21):2862 — *"Our findings indicate that sleep in industrial societies has not been reduced below a level that is normal for most of our species' evolutionary history."*
- **Wiessner 2014**, *PNAS* 111(39):14027 — *"Data from modern foragers cannot be projected back in the past."* To press: *"We can't tell about the past from the Bushmen."*
- **Samson et al. 2017**, *Proc R Soc B* 284(1858):20170967 — *"All individuals were simultaneously scored as asleep for a total of only 18 one-minute epochs over 20 nights"*; someone awake **99.8%** of epochs; median **8** awake. Chronotype × age: β = −0.23 ± 0.09, p = 0.01. n=33.

## Numbers used, with their denominators
- **Wiessner:** 174 conversations = **122 day / 52 night**, Botswana 1974. Day: economic **31%**, complaint/criticism **34%**, joking **16%**, stories **~6%**. Night: stories **81%**, conflict **7%**, economic **4%**. χ²=43.15, p<0.0001.
- **Yetish:** n=94, 1,165 days. Hadza 6.3±0.63 h; Tsimane 5.7–6.6; San 6.2–7.1. WASO **80.3 min**. Insomnia **1.5%/2.5%** chronic vs **10–30%** industrial. Two languages have no word for insomnia.
- **Hadza fire:** **295 of 436 nights = 67%** (Samson 2017, *AJPA*).
- **Eklund et al. 2017**, *Sci Rep* 7:2097 — reviewed anti-lion interventions 1990–2016. Fire is not among them.
- **Samson 2017 Madagascar**, *Am J Hum Biol* 29(4):e22979 — n=21, 292 days. **Segmented.** Sleep 6.5 h, efficiency 66%, WASO 162 min.

## ⚠️ TRAPS — the execution list
1. **The 81% is NOT "81% of night talk."** It's of *lengthy conversations involving many people*, and she deliberately sought large groups. Different denominator from the day figures. **Script says so out loud.**
2. **The ~6% day-stories figure is not in the paper's text** — it's read off Figure 1. Corroborated by the University of Utah press release. Script says "about six percent."
3. **Never use Wiessner to describe the Palaeolithic.** She forbids it. **Script quotes her forbidding it.**
4. Her 1974 fieldwork ran during "an unusually long dry and hungry spell." **Script says so.**
5. **"They slept straight through" is wrong** — WASO ≈ 80 min. What's absent is a consolidated *second block*.
6. **"Yetish killed segmented sleep" is wrong** — Samson 2017 Madagascar found segmentation. The fight is live; night length is the best synthesis on the table.
7. **Ekirch never claimed universality** — *"'Predominance'… has a distinct meaning quite apart from either 'universal' or 'ubiquitous'."* His waking interval is "up to an hour or so," NOT the 1–3 h (that's Wehr's lab).
8. **Wehr 1992 sample size is unsettled** — 7 / 15 / 16 across sources; the abstract states none. Avoid.
9. **NEVER assert fire deters predators.** No study exists. Even Ben-Dor & Barkai 2025, arguing hardest *for* it, can only cite a 1990s sediment interpretation.
10. **🚨 The Dunbar fireside-endorphin study DOES NOT EXIST.** It's a conflation of (a) Dunbar & Gowlett's theoretical "Fireside Chat" chapter, (b) Dunbar 2012's laughter/pain-threshold study which contains zero mentions of fire, (c) Dunbar's PNAS commentary. **Nearly went in the script. Cut.**
11. **Lynn 2014's "fire" was an *Ambient Fire* DVD on a computer screen**, subjects alone, no warmth or smell. Muted fire ≈ control. No sound-only control. Unexplained baseline anomaly. **Cut.**
12. **"Firelight doesn't suppress melatonin"** — modeled from spectral power distributions, **never measured in humans**. Say "should," or don't say it. **Cut.**
13. **Don't state a colour temperature for a campfire.** Only a candle flame (~1900 K, Shahnawaz 2021) is primary-sourced. The "1800–2200 K" range traces to lighting-industry marketing.
14. **"Campfires reset your circadian clock"** — Wright 2013 is n=8 and bundles sunlight + no electronics + fire; it credits the first two.
15. **State sample sizes on screen.** This audience *rewards* it.

## Banked for later episodes
- **Fire episode:** Wrangham's ~1.4 Myr gap (cooking at ~1.8 Ma vs habitual fire ~400 ka vs fire-*making* 400 ka). **Barnham pyrite — Davis et al. 2026, *Nature* 649(8097):631 — any 2026 fire script without this is obsolete.** Wonderwerk St. 11 (Marin-Monfort et al., *PLOS ONE*, 1 June 2026) is **1.79–1.07 Ma**, and its own authors say *opportunistic, no cooking, no fire-making* — do NOT say "1.8 Mya confirmed."
- **Sandgathe 2011:** Neandertals used **less** fire in **colder** periods — the opposite of intuition. Not consensus.
- **Cave-art episode:** Atxurra — three hearths **329 m** underground arranged to light an entire 12 m panel at once, smoke venting above the viewers; 85 motifs on 9.68 m². Medina-Alcaide et al. 2023. Also: torches burn 21–61 min; a deep-cave art visit was a **fuel-logistics problem**.
- **Storyteller episode:** Smith et al. 2017, *Nat Commun* 8:1853 — skilled storytellers had **0.53 more living offspring** (p=0.016, n=324), ~2× as likely to be a preferred camp-mate. ⚠️ Correlational by the authors' own admission, and **not a fire/night study**.
- **Moon:** Casiraghi et al. 2021, *Sci Adv* 7(5) — sleep swings 46–58 min across the lunar cycle, in Toba/Qom **and** 464 Seattle undergrads.
